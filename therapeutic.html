Therapeutic & Deep Tissue Massage

Focused, intentional work on the areas that are actually bothering you.

Therapeutic massage

Therapeutic massage aims to reduce stiffness, ease pain from tension and overuse, and help you move more freely. I'll ask what's going on, check how you move, and spend the session where it matters most instead of following a set routine.

It's a good fit if you have:

  • Neck, shoulder, or back tension that keeps coming back
  • Low back pain
  • Joint pain and stiffness, like in the hips, knees, or shoulders
  • Numbness, tingling, or sharp shooting pain in the hand and arm, or the glutes and leg, when tight muscles are pressing on a nerve
  • Wrist, forearm, and upper back strain from desk or computer work
  • Stiffness that limits your range of motion
  • Muscles that are still recovering from a strain or overuse injury
Nerve symptoms: please see a healthcare provider first if numbness or weakness is sudden, severe, or getting worse, or comes with loss of bladder or bowel control. Massage can help when the cause is muscular, but those symptoms need medical attention.

For desk workers

I spent years as a software developer before becoming an LMT, so I know firsthand what long days at a keyboard do to wrists, necks, and backs. Gamers and engineers are very welcome.

Deep tissue massage

Deep tissue uses slower, more sustained pressure to reach deeper layers of muscle and fascia. My pressure tends to run deep, and most of my clients come to me for exactly that. Deep doesn't have to mean painful, though. We'll keep the pressure at a level where your body can relax into it rather than brace against it, and you can ask me to adjust at any time.

Some soreness for a day or two afterward is normal, similar to after a good workout.

Specific areas I work with

Along with the usual neck, back, and shoulders, I have specific experience with abdominals, anterior (front of) neck, rotator cuff, and glutes. These areas are often overlooked but drive a lot of pain. Sensitive areas are only worked on with your written consent. See also abdominal & diaphragm massage.

After an acute injury, please see your healthcare provider first. Massage works best alongside medical care, not instead of it.
